# Discussion of Trend Figures for the Tuned Models

*This discussion refers to `Trend_TunedModels.png`, which compares the experimental 28-day compressive strength with predictions obtained using pooled 10-fold cross-validation and 100-repeat Monte Carlo cross-validation.*

The trend plots show that all four tuned ensemble models reproduce the main variation in 28-day compressive strength across the 136 mixtures. In particular, the predicted curves generally follow the experimental curve through both the low-strength observations at the beginning of the sequence and the higher-strength region after approximately sample 95. In this latter region, where the experimental strengths are comparatively clustered (approximately 45-67 MPa), the predicted and experimental curves are closely aligned for all models. This indicates that the models can reliably capture the response of mixtures represented by a relatively consistent range of input conditions.

The distinction between the models becomes clearer in the more variable middle portion of the figure, where adjacent mixtures exhibit abrupt increases and decreases in strength. XGBoost provides the closest overall correspondence with the experimental trend (10-fold CV R2 = 0.925), followed by CatBoost (R2 = 0.917) and Extra Trees (R2 = 0.903). Their predictions reproduce most peaks, valleys, and changes in direction, although the magnitudes of the sharpest changes are sometimes reduced. Random Forest has the lowest 10-fold CV performance among the tuned models (R2 = 0.875) and displays the most noticeable smoothing of the experimental signal. Its predictions remain close to the general pattern but tend to miss part of the amplitude of sudden local peaks and troughs.

All models show some difficulty at isolated extreme observations, including the low value near sample 37 and the high values near samples 47-52. At these points, predictions are pulled toward nearby, more typical strengths rather than fully matching the extreme experimental value. This behaviour is expected for tree-based ensemble models because predictions are formed from patterns represented in the training data; rare combinations of mixture variables are therefore more difficult to estimate accurately. Consequently, the deviations at these isolated points should be interpreted as local uncertainty in sparsely represented mix-design conditions, rather than as failure to reproduce the overall strength trend.

For each model, the 10-fold and Monte Carlo prediction curves are also broadly similar. The agreement across these two validation procedures indicates that the observed ranking is not dependent on a single resampling arrangement. XGBoost is consistently the strongest model in the figure, while CatBoost and Extra Trees remain competitive alternatives. Overall, the trend analysis supports the use of XGBoost as the preferred predictive model for 28-day compressive strength, while also showing that all tuned ensemble models capture the dominant experimental behaviour with reasonable accuracy.
