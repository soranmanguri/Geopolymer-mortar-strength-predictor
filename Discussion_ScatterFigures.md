# Discussion of Scatter Figures for the Tuned Models

*This discussion refers to `Scatter_TunedModels.png`, which plots experimental against predicted 28-day compressive strength for each tuned model, together with the identity line and a ±20% error band, using predictions from pooled 10-fold cross-validation and 100-repeat Monte Carlo cross-validation.*

Across all four tuned models, the majority of predictions fall within the ±20% error band and cluster closely around the identity line over the full experimental range (approximately 13-82 MPa). Agreement is tightest in the 40-65 MPa region, where the largest concentration of mixtures lies, indicating that all four models estimate strength most reliably for the most heavily represented range of mix-design conditions. At the lower end of the range (below approximately 20 MPa), the scatter appears proportionally wider relative to the narrowing error band, though the absolute deviations remain small; this is consistent with the reduced number of low-strength observations available for training rather than a systematic weakness specific to any one model.

The relative tightness of the point clouds follows the same ranking as the reported R2 values. XGBoost (R2 = 0.925) and CatBoost (R2 = 0.917) show the least scatter about the identity line, with almost all points contained within the ±20% band across the full range. Extra Trees (R2 = 0.903) shows a modest increase in scatter, particularly in the low-to-mid strength region. Random Forest (R2 = 0.875) displays the widest spread of the four models, with a visibly greater number of points approaching or crossing the ±20% boundary, most noticeably among mixtures below 40 MPa. This is consistent with the smoothing behaviour observed for Random Forest in the corresponding trend figure.

A small number of points fall outside the ±20% band in every model, generally corresponding to under-prediction rather than over-prediction: the predicted value sits below the identity line for a handful of mixtures in the 45-70 MPa experimental range. These recurring outliers align with the same isolated, sharply varying observations identified in the trend figure, supporting the interpretation that specific mix-design combinations are inherently harder to predict, likely due to limited representation in the training data, rather than indicating a systematic bias in any model.

The close overlap between the 10-fold CV and Monte Carlo markers in every panel further confirms that these patterns are stable across resampling procedures rather than an artefact of a single validation split. Taken together, the scatter plots corroborate the trend-figure findings: XGBoost provides the most consistent predictions across the full strength range, followed closely by CatBoost, while Random Forest, despite remaining broadly reliable, exhibits the greatest deviation from the identity line among the tuned models.
